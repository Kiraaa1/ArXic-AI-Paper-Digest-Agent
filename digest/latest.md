### 1. An Empirical Study of Harness Design for Coding Agents
**Authors:** Run-Ze Fan, Zihao Zhang, Simin Ma, Yebowen Hu, Shouju Wang, Kaiqiang Song, Fei Liu, Hamed Zamani, Xiaoyang Wang
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20804v1
**Summary:** The paper investigates how different components of coding harnesses affect the performance of autonomous coding agents, focusing on aspects like planning and context management. By analyzing various configurations across multiple models, the authors find that effective context management is crucial, especially under tight constraints, and specific strategies like staging elision improve efficiency. The study offers a modular framework that can guide future harness design by highlighting the interplay between components and their impact on software engineering tasks.

### 2. JEPA-Anything: Learning Predictive Models across Different Worlds
**Authors:** Taoyong Cui, Zhongyao Wang, Xinyue Xu, Weiyang Liu, Zhaochen Yu, Yuying Zhang, Qiang Gao, Mengyue Yang, Wanli Ouyang, Pheng Ann Heng, Yingcheng Wu, Zhenfei Yin, Ling Yang
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20800v1
**Summary:** The paper presents JEPA-Anything, a novel framework designed to create predictive models that work across diverse domains, such as vision and biology, by utilizing a method called orthogonal predictive factorization (OPF). This approach allows the model to break down predictions into complementary factors and learn them efficiently, leading to significant improvements in forecasting tasks, including a 34.8% reduction in prediction errors for single interventions in a simulated environment. The results demonstrate the model's effectiveness in generating accurate predictions and facilitating scientific discovery through intervention predictions in real-world biological contexts.

### 3. PosteriorBench: From Point Estimates to Posterior Matching in Evaluating Generative Inverse Solvers
**Authors:** Jiachen Yao, Zi-Siang Hsu, Xi Deng, Aditi Gupta, Xin Ju, Sally M Benson, Gege Wen, Anima Anandkumar
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20794v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the inadequacy of existing evaluations for generative models used in solving inverse problems, which often focus only on producing a single plausible solution instead of capturing the full uncertainty distribution of possible solutions. To tackle this, the authors introduce PosteriorBench, a benchmark that assesses generative inverse solvers on their ability to recover the true posterior distributions across four specific physics-based problems, using high-fidelity reference posteriors generated by sophisticated sampling techniques. Key findings indicate that many current solvers fail to match the true distributions, while improvements can be made through the use of neural operators and careful adjustments to guidance and generation noise.

### 4. RetireOPD: Self-Retiring On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning
**Authors:** Yan Yu, Zhengxi Lu, Yizhou Liu, Yichen Pan, Aozhe Wang, Qipeng Chen, Hua Yang, Wenqi Zhang, Weiming Lu, Qianglong Chen, Yongliang Shen
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20784v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of improving multi-turn agents in reinforcement learning by enhancing the training process through self on-policy distillation (OPD), where a teacher model provides guidance to a student model. The introduced method, RetireOPD, optimizes a separate skill-conditioned teacher before allowing the student to autonomously determine when to rely on the teacher's guidance, improving performance significantly. The results demonstrate that RetireOPD increases success rates in various benchmark tasks compared to traditional RL methods and even outperforms the teacher model in several scenarios.

### 5. Harm Laundering in GPT Models: Evidence That Gender Discrimination Is Transformed Rather Than Reduced Across Safety-Trained Generations
**Authors:** Sarah Wyer, Sue Black, Noura Al Moubayed
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20779v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the issue of gender discrimination in large language models (LLMs), specifically how safety evaluations may misleadingly suggest that harm is being reduced when, in fact, it is simply being transformed into less explicit forms—an effect termed "harm laundering." The authors analyze 450,000 gender-directed outputs from different iterations of OpenAI's GPT models and find that while toxic content targeting women declines, representational harm actually increases for men, creating a disparity in topic diversity and framing. They propose a formal definition of harm laundering and a detection protocol for assessing generative models beyond standard toxicity scores.

### 6. GeoAAC: Geometry-Based Adaptive Action Chunking from Denoising Trajectories in VLA Policies
**Authors:** Xin Chen, Sen Chen, Yujuan Ding, Jian Liu, Guoqing Wang, Wei Ye, Heng Tao Shen, Yi Bin
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20776v1
**Summary:** The paper introduces GeoAAC, a novel method for adaptive action chunking in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies that dynamically adjusts the action horizon based on the reliability of action predictions during task execution. By leveraging geometric information from Flow Matching denoising trajectories, GeoAAC enables more precise control by tailoring the action duration to the current task demands. The results demonstrate significant improvements in both simulation and real-world tasks, with a success rate increase from 53.3% to 74.4%.

### 7. Semantic Action Graph: A Shared Representation for Agent Grounding and Human Interpretation of Sports Highlights
**Authors:** Tica Lin, Deepak Chandran, Gauri Jagatap, Chen Chen, Andrea Fanelli, David Gunawan, Josh Kimball
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20768v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of generating and narrating sports video highlights in a way that is understandable and controllable by viewers. It introduces the semantic action graph, a structured representation that breaks down sports matches into key components like performers, actions, and their relationships, allowing both automated highlight generation and viewer interaction. The initial implementation, SportSAGE, received positive feedback from soccer fans, indicating that the approach effectively supports both automated content creation and human comprehension.

### 8. Calibrated RF-Fingerprinting Under Interference With Heterogeneous Transmission Protocols
**Authors:** Tariq Abdul-Quddoos, Xiangfang Li, Lijun Qian
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20765v1
**Summary:** This paper addresses the challenge of accurately identifying multiple overlapping wireless transmitters in environments with co-channel interference, which is often overlooked in existing RF-fingerprinting research. The authors formulated the problem as a multi-label classification task and utilized a 1D convolutional neural network, introducing a calibration method to improve the reliability of detection. Their results indicate high accuracy rates, reaching up to 97%, demonstrating the method's effectiveness in real-world scenarios with various transmission protocols.

### 9. Agile-WAM: An Agile Tactile World Action Model for Contact-Rich Robot Control
**Authors:** Hanchu Zhou, Brendan Lynch, Raman Goyal, Dechen Gao, Begum Kasap, Boqi Zhao, Junshan Zhang
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20761v1
**Summary:** The paper presents Agile-WAM, a new approach for robot control in environments where contact dynamics are critical, which improves the efficiency of predicting robot actions and future states byusing both visual and tactile information. By introducing a method that accounts for the different timescales of visual and tactile signals, Agile-WAM achieves notably faster inference times and better performance in real-world manipulation tasks, demonstrating a 29.4% increase in success rates compared to existing methods. This approach offers a more agile and effective solution for precise robot control in contact-rich scenarios.

### 10. Prediction-Powered Smoothing and Validation for Disaggregated AI Evaluation
**Authors:** Sho Kawano, Zehang Richard Li, Paul A. Parker
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20758v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of accurately evaluating AI systems across different domains, given that performance can vary significantly and extensive testing is costly. The authors introduce a method called prediction-powered smoothing (PP-S), which enhances estimation by incorporating information across similar domains, leading to more precise performance estimates. Key results demonstrate that their approach improves both point and interval estimation compared to traditional methods, achieving accurate error estimations while maintaining a similar selection quality to independent validation samples.
