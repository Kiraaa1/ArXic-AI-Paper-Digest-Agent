## 2026-11-27

### 1. Interpretable and Accurate Hybrid Decision Trees with Selective Case-Based Splits
**Authors:** Cristiano Landi, Alessio Cascione, Riccardo Guidotti
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34887v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of improving the predictive performance and interpretability of decision trees by integrating both feature-based and case-based split strategies. The authors propose a hybrid decision tree architecture that allows for more informative splits by selectively utilizing feature subsets, leading to more accurate models. Their experiments demonstrate that this approach enhances decision tree performance, achieving accuracy comparable to ensemble methods while maintaining simpler tree structures.

### 2. Stranger Things: When Objects Appear Without Their Typical Neighbors
**Authors:** Siddhartha Gairola, Jiahao Xie, Anna Kukleva, Francesco Locatello, Bernt Schiele
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34881v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the problem of typicality bias in object recognition models, where models misidentify objects based on their common co-occurrences with other objects rather than their own features. To tackle this, the authors introduced a diagnostic framework and a new benchmark called STRANGE-Bench to evaluate the bias, along with a novel data augmentation technique known as Anchor-Atypicality Sampling (AAS), which shifts training data towards less typical object pairings. The key finding is that AAS significantly reduces typicality bias by up to 23 percentage points in certain scenarios, enhancing the models’ robustness without compromising overall performance.

### 3. How Much Do Coding Agents Over-Edit, and Why? An Empirical Study of Unneeded Edits in Repository-Level Agent Patches
**Authors:** Aakash Goel, Benjamin Steenhoek, Yu Hu
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34875v1
**Summary:** The paper investigates the phenomenon of over-editing in patches generated by coding agents compared to human developers, aiming to determine the necessity of extra code changes made by these agents. The authors introduce a method called Delta Residue Attribution (DRA) to analyze the origins and necessity of changes within agent-generated patches from GitHub Copilot. Key findings show that while agents produce larger patches with many additional edits, their code is generally as necessary as that from developers, highlighting the potential for refining agent outputs through better training and selective pruning.

### 4. Shape-Aware Three-Way Merge for RDF Knowledge Graphs: The schema defines the conflict: an implementation and evaluation
**Authors:** Steve Brown
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34870v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenges of merging RDF knowledge graphs, specifically focusing on conflicts arising from schema-defined constraints. The authors propose a three-way merging approach that uses SHACL shapes to identify and resolve conflicts by applying set algebra to canonical triples. Key results show that this shape-aware method effectively identified 33 true conflict cases without losing any data, outperforming a traditional context-overlap method while significantly reducing the number of queries required to resolve these conflicts.

### 5. A Survey of Security, Privacy, and Ethics of Large Language Models in Telecom
**Authors:** Joy Bose, Srinivas Adyapak
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34868v1
**Summary:** This paper addresses the privacy, security, and ethical challenges associated with the use of Large Language Models (LLMs) in telecommunications. It provides a comprehensive survey that introduces a specialized taxonomy linking these concerns to specific telecom use cases and identifies key research challenges, such as managing privacy for longitudinal data and ensuring compliance across borders. The contribution lies in offering a framework for understanding and mitigating risks related to LLM deployment in the telecom sector.

### 6. RePace: Reasoning about Demonstration Speedup for Faster Vision-Language-Action Policies
**Authors:** Insup Lee, Hojin Jeon, Sunguk Shin, Seoungbin Bae, Taekwan Kim, Jinwoo Shin, Dongsu Han
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34864v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of slow vision-language-action (VLA) policies inherited from human demonstrations by proposing an offline reasoning framework called RePace. This framework intelligently determines how much to accelerate different parts of a demonstration by analyzing the effects of various speedup factors on the task execution. The results show that RePace improves task execution efficiency, achieving better success rates and faster completion times compared to existing baselines in robotic tasks.

### 7. Focus Only on What's Important: Training-Free Focus Maps for Frozen Vision Transformers in Long-Video Object Segmentation
**Authors:** Harsha Karipineni, Rajesh Reddy Chintha
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34863v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of efficiently segmenting objects in long video sequences using vision transformers, which can be computationally expensive. The authors propose a training-free method that employs focus maps to enhance segmentation accuracy in critical areas while reducing computational load elsewhere, minimizing the impact of fast object motion. Their approach outperforms existing methods by achieving higher accuracy with fewer computational resources, demonstrating significant efficiency gains in long-video object segmentation tasks.

### 8. BOP-Refer: Benchmark for Monocular 3D and 2D Object Grounding
**Authors:** Tomas Hodan, Vineet Bhat, Martin Sundermeyer, Jonathan Tremblay, Yash Patel, Médéric Fourmy, Stephen Tyree, Junwen Huang, Varun Burde, Martin Cífka, Eric Brachmann, Vincent Lepetit, Stan Birchfield, Jiri Matas, Carsten Rother
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34862v1
**Summary:** The paper presents BOP-Refer, a benchmark designed to evaluate monocular 3D and 2D object grounding using everyday and industrial objects. By combining various datasets with generated and human-verified referring expressions, the authors created accurate ground-truth annotations for object localization. The results show that while the best-performing model, Gemini 4 Carbon, achieves a decent 72.6% accuracy in 2D object grounding, it struggles significantly with 3D grounding at only 12.4% accuracy, pointing to issues in estimating object sizes in three-dimensional space.

### 9. Model Weights, Airlocks and Sandboxes: A Call for Technical Validation of Re-identification Tests under the EDPB Anonymisation Guidelines
**Authors:** Rickard Brännvall, Henrik Forsgren, Erik Ylipää, Aslak Felin, Michael Popoff, Susanne Stenberg
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34859v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of validating re-identification tests for anonymized data under the European Data Protection Board's guidelines, which currently lack specific design and validation criteria. The authors propose a framework that includes secure processing environments for data review, distinguishing among different types of evidence supporting re-identification test results. They emphasize the need for these tests to be rigorously validated as reliable measurement methods, complete with clarity on detection limits, bias, and uncertainty.

### 10. From Prediction to Decision: Decision-Focused Fine-Tuning of Autoregressive Tabular Transformers for Cost-Sensitive Financial Events
**Authors:** Ashkan Golgoon, Giacomo Domeniconi, Samuel Atkins, Sergio Servantez, Zain Khandwala
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34858v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of making effective financial decisions based on predictions of events like fraud, where the costs of errors are not equal. The authors propose a method for fine-tuning autoregressive transformers to directly optimize decision-making by maximizing expected utility according to a specified cost matrix. Their approach enhances model performance by aligning it more closely with real-world costs, while still maintaining the benefits of the pretrained model's calibration and generative capabilities.
