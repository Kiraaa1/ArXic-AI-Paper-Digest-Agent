### 1. VBVR-Pro: A Scalable and Verifiable Suite for Native Visual Reasoning

Müller, Anyi Rao, Quan Wang, Ziwei Liu, Dahua Lin, Lei Yang, Hokin Deng, Zhongang Cai
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26105v1
**Summary:** The paper introduces VBVR-Pro, a novel framework aimed at enhancing native visual reasoning by creating a scalable and verifiable environment for training models on visual tasks. It features 300 procedurally generated tasks, reliable evaluation methods, and allows for in-depth analysis of different visual generation modalities. Key findings indicate that video generation excels in tasks requiring spatiotemporal tracking, while the framework significantly improves model performance across various visual reasoning benchmarks.

### 2. A Visual Dependence-Aware Framework for Multimodal Unsupervised Continual Post-Training
**Authors:** Kaichen Li, Zhilin Zhu, Jianhao Huang, Zhengqin Lai, Baochen Xiong, Zibo Shao, Yaguang Song, Linhui Xiao, Xiaoshan Yang, Changsheng Xu
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26095v1
**Summary:** This paper addresses the challenge of enabling multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to continually learn from streaming unlabeled data without forgetting previously acquired knowledge. The authors propose a Visual Dependence-Aware (VDA) framework that incorporates a method for optimizing visual dependence to prevent cross-modal forgetting and enhance learning of new tasks. Their approach demonstrates significant improvements in maintaining stability on older tasks while fostering adaptability for new tasks through extensive experimental validation.

### 3. MyoMechanix: Biomechanically-Grounded Compositional Skilled Activity Understanding and Coaching
**Authors:** Hao Yin, Paritosh Parmar, Lijun Gu, Lin Xu, Tianxiao Guo, Xiujin Liu, Tianyou Zheng, Yang Zhang, Weiwei Fu
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26094v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the limitations of existing action quality assessment methods, which typically rely on visual data and ignore important muscle mechanics. The authors introduce MyoMechanix, a multimodal system that incorporates various physiological signals along with visual data to provide detailed feedback on skilled activities. Key contributions include the development of a comprehensive dataset and a novel reasoning engine, CUBIST, which enables better error attribution and interpretable assessments, achieving state-of-the-art performance in various tasks related to understanding and coaching physical activities.

### 4. Agentic Autoresearch for Cell-Edge Power Control: Radically Redefining the Researcher's Role
**Authors:** Ahmad Khan, Akram Bin Sediq, Sara Azadegi Naeini, Raviraj S. Adve
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26093v1
**Summary:** This paper addresses the complex problem of power control in wireless networks, which involves optimizing resource allocation for cell-edge users. The authors introduce an autonomous AI agent that autonomously designs and improves machine learning algorithms through a process called autoresearch, allowing it to automatically adjust various aspects of the model. The key contribution is that, after conducting 81 experiments, the AI agent achieved 99.5% of the performance of a sophisticated benchmark while using significantly fewer resources, discovering a general solution applicable across different network configurations.

### 5. PlanSightRAG: A Visual-First Multimodal RAG for Automating Question Answering and Compliance Checking for Civil Standard Plans
**Authors:** Nabaraj Subedi, Shuvo Dip Datta, Ahmed Abdelaty, Shivanand Venkanna Sheshappanavar
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26091v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of automating compliance checking for civil infrastructure by moving beyond traditional OCR methods that neglect essential visual details in 2D plans. It introduces PlanSightRAG, a Visual-First Multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework that directly analyzes plan imagery and achieves high retrieval accuracy. Key results include a 91.47% recall on zero-shot retrieval tasks and the ability to autonomously extract compliance rules from specifications, showcasing significant advancements in automating this engineering task.

### 6. Finding and using interpretable latents in a neutrino foundation model with sparse autoencoders
**Authors:** Raphaël Bonnet-Guerrini, Johann Ioannou-Nikolaides, Inar Timiryasov, Vincenzo Piuri
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26090v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of interpreting a neural network model used for neutrino direction reconstruction from IceCube data. By applying sparse autoencoders, the authors identify physical concepts that the model has learned, revealing important latent features. They demonstrate that integrating these interpretable features into a new uncertainty prediction head significantly improves the model's angular resolution, showcasing the utility of mechanistic interpretability in enhancing performance in particle physics tasks.

### 8. TraceML: An Empirical Analysis of Human-Agent Planning in Machine Learning Development
**Authors:** Jiarui Yan, Weiwei Sun, Sijie Li, Wenhan Li, Yiming Yang
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26086v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of large language models struggling with autonomous machine learning development, particularly in comparison to human competitors. It introduces TraceML, a framework that analyzes and compares the development processes of human and agent participants in Kaggle competitions by tracking code versions, actions taken, and their outcomes. The key finding is that while human experts effectively manage a diverse set of strategies and can pivot their approaches, AI agents tend to operate in a limited, repetitive manner, and although minor adjustments improve their performance, the fundamental difference in planning flexibility remains unaddressed.

### 9. ICON Decomposition: Multivariate Concept-Level Explanations of Deep Representations for Model Auditing
**Authors:** Roshan Prakash Rane, Marco Simnacher, Manuel Pfeuffer, Marc-Andre Schulz, Nys Tjade Siegel, Maximilian Dreyer, Frederik Pahde, Wojciech Samek, Sonja Greven, Kerstin Ritter
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26083v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the problem of deep neural networks relying on misleading shortcuts in their training data, which can lead to incorrect predictions. The authors propose a new method called ICON decomposition that accurately measures the importance of various concepts in a neural network by considering their interactions instead of evaluating them in isolation. Key results show that ICON outperforms existing approaches in identifying the relevant concepts in multiple medical imaging models and provides clearer explanations of model behavior.

### 10. SwarmWorld: Stigmergic technological evolution in societies of language-model agents
**Authors:** Subhadeep Pal, Fiona Y. Wang, Markus J. Buehler
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26081v1
**Summary:** The paper investigates how decentralized language-model agents can collaboratively develop technologies in a shared environment without predefined roles or direct communication. By using a simulated world where agents explore and construct artifacts, the study demonstrates that these agents can self-organize into societies that produce a more diverse and resilient technological portfolio compared to isolated approaches. It highlights the importance of physical interactions and cultural mechanisms in enhancing collaboration and technological evolution among agents.
