## 2026-10-20

### 1. Text-to-Image Generative Modeling Without Language Pretraining
**Authors:** Keshigeyan Chandrasegaran, Yue Zhao, Armin W. Thomas, Ehsan Adeli, Jiajun Wu, Juan Carlos Niebles, Li Fei-Fei, Michael Poli
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26863v1
**Summary:** The paper investigates whether large-scale language pretraining is necessary for effective text-to-image (T2I) generation. The authors present Halcyon, a multimodal autoregressive model trained entirely from scratch on a dataset of 100M image-text pairs, which achieves superior performance compared to models using extensive language pretraining while requiring significantly less computational resources. The key finding is that robust T2I systems can be developed without relying on internet-scale language models.

### 2. OpenVLHarness: Real World Visual Agentic Tool-Use with Multimodal Memory
**Authors:** Yu Zhou, Cheng-Fu Yang, Rui Sun, Di Wu, Sibo Peng, Mingyang Zhang, Zhen Yang, Ruiyi Zhang, Nanyun Peng, Zhe Gan, Kai-Wei Chang
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26860v1
**Summary:** OpenVLHarness addresses the challenges of visual reasoning tasks that require external evidence and tool use, which are not effectively managed by large language models alone. The framework integrates a persistent multimodal memory and a capability layer to manage and interpret various tools and data, allowing for iterative evidence gathering and composition. Key results show that OpenVLHarness significantly improves performance on 22 benchmarks, achieving up to 12.6 points higher than models using no tools, while being more efficient in terms of model-token usage.

### 3. Can It Actually Work? Synthesizing Functional CAD Mechanisms
**Authors:** Wenqian Zhang, Yangyi Huang, Zigeng Xu, Weiyang Liu, Zhen Liu
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26861v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of ensuring that CAD designs generated by language models actually function as intended, rather than just appearing visually plausible. The authors introduce a framework called FunCAD-Harness that translates abstract design requirements into specific tests, using physical simulations to guide iterative design improvements. Their experiments show that this approach significantly improves the ability of designs to meet functional requirements, revealing issues that traditional visual inspections might miss.

### 4. Reinforcement Learning with Visually Attributable On-Policy Self-Distillation
**Authors:** Shiao Xie, Siyu Chen, Jianwei Lv, Xiandong Li, Yujin Wang
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26856v1
**Summary:** This paper addresses the challenge of accurately localizing errors in visual reasoning within reinforcement learning frameworks that use multimodal rewards. The authors propose a novel approach called Visual-Attribution Gating (VAG), which enhances on-policy self-distillation by ensuring that corrections from a privileged teacher are grounded in visual evidence, thereby avoiding misleading updates influenced by non-visual factors. The experimental results demonstrate that VAG significantly improves performance across multiple benchmarks, achieving state-of-the-art results in visual reasoning tasks.

### 5. CoopUQ: Robust Multi-Robot Social Navigation via Uncertainty-Aware Cooperative Forecasting
**Authors:** Zehao Wang, Jianpeng Yao, Yuping Wang, Yiping Li, Jiaqi Li, Zhixu Li, Kanghoon Lee, Jinfeng Wang, Cong Liu, Zhaowei Tan, Jinkyoo Park, Jiachen Li
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26852v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of safe and efficient navigation for multiple robots in environments shared with humans, particularly under conditions where accurate predictions can become difficult, such as occlusions and out-of-distribution scenarios. The authors introduce CoopUQ, a framework that enhances cooperative occupancy forecasting using uncertainty-aware methods, allowing robots to make better navigation decisions while minimizing risks. Experimental results show that CoopUQ outperforms existing methods in both simulation and real-world settings, improving prediction accuracy and overall safety.

### 6. Where is Physical RSI capability from? An Observational Scaling Law of LLM-as-policy
**Authors:** Zekai Li, Jiayi Yu, Jinlu Zhang, Luyang Niu, Zekun Qi, Jun Zheng, Xuchuan Chen, Dairu Liu, Yintianrun Zhang, He Wang, Li Yi
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26848v1
**Summary:** This paper explores the mechanisms and performance scaling of physical recursive self-improvement (RSI) in varying visual language models (VLMs) through a novel evaluation framework called PhysRSI-eval. By assessing these models across multiple manipulation tasks, the study introduces the Physical Barrel Hypothesis, which suggests that different capabilities affect distinct aspects of improvement. Key findings indicate that high performance does not always correlate with early or efficient success, highlighting the complexity of how agents can adapt and learn from their experiences.

### 7. WeAct: Dynamic Weight Switching as Agent Actions
**Authors:** Rui Shi, Xunjian Yin, Jiacheng Sang, Zichuan Liu, Shuyan Zhou
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26847v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the issue of catastrophic forgetting in long-horizon agents that must adapt to various tasks during execution. The authors propose WeAct, which utilizes dynamic weight switching through independent capability-specific adapters, allowing agents to select the appropriate skills on-the-fly without altering a shared frozen backbone. Their approach improves accuracy significantly—by 6.2% compared to full fine-tuning and 27.5% over static methods—while also enabling modular continual adaptation of the agents' capabilities.

### 8. Train An Imaginative Agent
**Authors:** Jiaxin Ge, Junyi Zhang, Tianning Chai, Hanjun Yoo, Chenyang Wang, Xiuyu Li, Weijia Shi, XuDong Wang, Sewon Min, Luke Zettlemoyer, Trevor Darrell, Haiwen Feng
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26845v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of preventing an agent from making irreversible mistakes in environments like Sokoban by teaching it to predict the consequences of its actions before acting. The authors introduce a framework called Agentic Imagination (Agima), which trains a single model to propose actions, visualize their potential outcomes, and reason about them for effective decision-making. Key results show that Agima significantly improves problem-solving capabilities, achieving a 40% success rate on complex Sokoban puzzles, compared to just 2% with traditional planning methods, after incorporating reinforcement learning.

### 9. AnyGoal: On World Model Generalization Beyond Expert Rollouts
**Authors:** Heejeong Nam, Divyat Mahajan, Quentin Le Lidec, Randall Balestriero, David Klindt
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26844v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of how well world models can generalize their learned dynamics to new combinations of state and action factors that were not extensively represented in expert rollouts. The authors introduce AnyGoal, a benchmark that evaluates goal-shifted task variants in common world-model environments, and they demonstrate that existing models frequently struggle with these recombinations. Through their framework and analysis, they provide insights into the limitations of current world models in generalizing to new planning scenarios, highlighting areas for future improvement.

### 10. LatComm: Reusable Context Compression for Latent Communication between Large Language Models
**Authors:** Huawei Lin, Jinting Liu, Tan Yu, Weijie Zhao
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26840v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the inefficiency of large language models (LLMs) that repeatedly process the same context for different questions, which can be computationally expensive. To solve this, the authors introduce LatComm, a method that compresses context into a reusable format and allows a different LLM to access relevant information quickly for each question without re-processing the entire context. The results show that LatComm significantly improves accuracy on multiple-choice questions and speeds up the answering process by reducing input size dramatically.
