## 2026-09-04

### 1. Compile by Training: Turning Natural-Language Specifications into Local Neural Functions
**Authors:** Yuntian Deng, Pengyu Nie, Stuart Shieber
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04199v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of implementing complex text functions that are difficult to define with rules and costly to execute using large remote models. It introduces a method called "compile by training," which generates a small, reusable neural function from natural language specifications by creating task-specific examples during compilation. This approach achieves an impressive semantic accuracy of 83.6% on a challenging benchmark, despite requiring more time during compilation compared to traditional fast compilers.

### 2. Clean Engineering, Unstable Measurement: A Preregistered Reliability Failure of Black-Box LLM Observers on Shared Endpoints
**Authors:** Haoyaun Zhu, Jie Zhang
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04198v1
**Summary:** This paper addresses the reliability of black-box language model judges, which are used to evaluate training data and model outputs. The authors conducted two preregistered audits with nearly 53,000 requests to assess whether the same input yields consistent rankings over time, revealing significant measurement instability. Key findings indicate that the models produced markedly inconsistent results, suggesting that standard evaluation practices on shared endpoints are flawed, highlighting the need for improved measurement protocols in future evaluations.

### 3. ESPO: Error-Structured Prompt Optimization via Diagnose, Diversify, and Stabilize
**Authors:** Lihao Liu, Peng Tang, Kunwar Yashraj Singh, Shabnam Ghadar
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04197v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the problem of "prompt bloat" in evolutionary prompt optimizers, where prompts become excessively long without improving accuracy. The authors introduce ESPO (Error-Structured Prompt Optimization), which streamlines prompt optimization into three phases: diagnosing errors, generating diverse candidates, and selecting stable prompts. The results show that ESPO outperforms the previous state-of-the-art method, improving accuracy by 3.76 percentage points while producing prompts that are 47% shorter, and achieving the best performance across multiple models and benchmarks.

### 4. Legibility is Not Interpretability: Comparing Judged and Actual Importance in Chain-Of-Thought Reasoning
**Authors:** Kevin Du, Alexander Hoyle, Laura Ruis, Acyr Locatelli
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04194v1
**Summary:** The paper investigates whether the text in chain-of-thought reasoning from language models truly reflects the importance of each reasoning step in driving towards a correct answer. By estimating the advantage of each step using Monte Carlo simulations, the authors found that while advanced language models can identify some significant steps better than random chance, they still struggle to fully capture step importance. This highlights that the apparent clarity of reasoning traces does not equate to genuine interpretability, particularly in the context of rewarding model processes.

### 5. One Editor, Many Edits: A Unified Training-Free Framework for Diverse Video Editing
**Authors:** Adheesh Sunil Juvekar, Onkar Kishor Susladkar, Kiet A. Nguyen, Muntasir Wahed, Nabeel Bashir, Xiaona Zhou, Tianjiao Yu, Vedant Shah, Ismini Lourentzou
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04190v1
**Summary:** The paper presents EditVid, a training-free framework designed to enhance video editing by supporting various editing styles, such as instruction-guided and reference-guided edits. It utilizes advanced techniques like sparse causal memory and token injection to maintain coherence and identity across edits. EditVid significantly outperforms existing methods, achieving a 78.16 accuracy on the FiVE benchmark and showing strong user preference in comparative studies.

### 6. Robust PAC Learning of Concurrent Stochastic Games
**Authors:** Angel Y. He, David Parker
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04189v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of learning strategies in general-sum concurrent stochastic games (CSGs) where the outcomes are uncertain and Nash equilibria may not exist. The authors present a novel PAC learning framework that uses robust methods to compute an approximate Nash equilibrium with optimal social welfare, while also certifying cases when no exact equilibrium is possible. Their approach achieves efficient sample complexity and demonstrates strong empirical performance on benchmark games, confirming the theoretical guarantees provided.

### 7. Seeing Before Synthesizing: VLM-Guided Transition Event Discovery for Weakly-Supervised Dense Video Captioning
**Authors:** Ye-Chan Kim, Seunghee Choi, SeungJu Cha, Si-Woo Kim, Hwiseon Kim, Hyungee Kim, Dong-Jin Kim
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04183v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of weakly-supervised dense video captioning, where the goal is to identify and describe multiple events in untrimmed videos using only a set of event-level captions. The authors introduce the Seeing Before Synthesizing (SBS) framework, which improves the process by using visually grounded linguistic guidance generated by a vision-language model (VLM) to enhance the identification of transition moments between events. Their approach achieves state-of-the-art results in both event captioning and localization on benchmark datasets.

### 8. Knowledge Acquisition During Pre-training? Large Language Models Learn Better With Auxiliary Views
**Authors:** Joseph Lee, Yidi Huang, Dokyoon Kim, Shu Yang, Li Shen
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04180v1
**Summary:** The paper investigates how large language models (LLMs) gain knowledge during pre-training and proposes that using auxiliary views, or different reformulations of knowledge, enhances this learning process. Through controlled experiments, the authors find that incorporating these auxiliary views—even when the teacher model is weaker—improves knowledge acquisition, particularly in factual recall. Ultimately, this research highlights the importance of data diversity and the role of supplementary knowledge representations in effective pre-training.

### 9. A Computationally Feasible Framework for Causal Probabilistic Explanation
**Authors:** Rafal Urbaniak, Sam Witty, Daniel Waxman, Andy Zane, Poorva Garg, Emily Bunnapradist, Sankaran Vaidyanathan, Jack Feser, Drew Lehe, Eli Bingham
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04177v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of providing clear explanations for specific outcomes in probabilistic models by bridging the gap between actual causality, which is limited to small models, and scalable methods that often overlook causal structures. The authors introduce a new framework called Probabilistic Causal Impact (PCI), which reformulates causal explanation as a probabilistic estimation problem solvable through Monte Carlo methods, offering more accurate and nuanced explanations. PCI is shown to be effective in various scenarios, validating its consistency with existing theories while also demonstrating scalability to complex real-world applications.
