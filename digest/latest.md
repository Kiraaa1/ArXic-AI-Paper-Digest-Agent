## 2026-09-28

### 1. Learning to Stop without Learning to Stop: Self-Supervised Confidence Training Improves Reasoning Efficiency
**Authors:** Parsa Hosseini, Akasha Tigalappanavara, Sumit Nawathe, Chenrui Fan, Sourya Basu, Genta Indra Winata, Anirban Das, Soheil Feizi, Nima Chitsazan
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31619v1
**Summary:** This paper addresses the problem of long reasoning traces in models, which lead to high computational costs during inference. The authors propose a self-supervised training method that fine-tunes models to predict their confidence in answers at various reasoning stages, without directly optimizing for shorter reasoning. The key finding is that this confidence-based approach can significantly reduce the number of tokens generated by the models—up to 25%—while maintaining accuracy, demonstrating that improving reasoning efficiency can arise from learning about metacognition rather than enforcing specific efficiency constraints.

### 2. Gap-free Differentially Private PCA for Gaussian Data
**Authors:** Alina Ene, Huy L. Nguyen
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31614v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of performing principal component analysis (PCA) on Gaussian datasets while ensuring differential privacy. The authors present a novel algorithm that achieves this without any accuracy gaps, meaning it can effectively maintain privacy while accurately capturing the underlying data structure. The key contribution is a gap-free approach that enhances the utility of PCA in privacy-sensitive applications.

### 3. First-Order Stationarity of Reverse Diffusions
**Authors:** Zhifeng Chen, Chenyang Jiang, Yazhen Wang
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31612v1
**Summary:** This paper explores the relationship between optimization and sampling in the context of diffusion models, focusing on the first-order theory for reverse-time diffusion processes. The authors demonstrate that these reverse diffusion flows can effectively reduce Fisher divergences when certain conditions on the noise are met, a feature not found in other methods. They also provide averaged bounds for first-order stationarity in sampling, showing that similar concepts from nonconvex optimization can apply, though the guarantees are local and pertain more to score consistency than global outcomes.

### 4. Statistical attribute alignment for black-box generative AI via output post-processing
**Authors:** Kevin Jiang, Morgane Austern, Edgar Dobriban, Jason M. Klusowski
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31607v1
**Summary:** This paper addresses the challenge of aligning outputs from generative AI systems with user-defined attribute distributions, such as fairness across gender or race in generated content. The authors propose algorithms that post-process the outputs of the AI to ensure the desired statistical alignment while minimizing the number of queries to the model. Their experiments demonstrate that these algorithms effectively improve the alignment of generated outputs, offering a complementary approach to existing prompting methods.

### 5. User Model Extraction via Belief Self-Distillation
**Authors:** Ali Holmov, Yiran Huang, Kirill Bykov, Zeynep Akata
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31603v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of understanding and manipulating the implicit beliefs that large language models (LLMs) form about their users. The authors introduce a method called Belief Self-Distillation (BSD), which allows these models to learn and extract compact user representations from conversations without external labels. A key finding is that these beliefs can be directly modified to change the model's responses, highlighting the importance of inferred user intent in decision-making processes and providing insights for enhancing AI safety.

### 6. New LoRA Skills Should Read but Never Write
**Authors:** Zeyan Li, Panqi Yang, Qirong Guo, Shengda Zhuo, SIyuan Qiu, Hu Xu, Chun Li, Jianfeng Xu
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31600v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of merging multiple independently trained low-rank adapters (LoRA) into a single model without losing the performance of existing skills. The authors introduce a method called READ (Read-only Expansion of Adapter Deltas) that reforms each adapter to maintain its updates while ensuring that new skills can only read from, not write over, the existing skills' outputs. This approach significantly improves performance across various benchmarks, surpassing existing methods with notable gains, particularly in tasks like SuperGLUE.

### 7. Common-Mode Collapse and Recovery in Direct Feedback Alignment
**Authors:** Varun Reddy, Bernardo L. Sabatini, Houman Safaai
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31589v1
**Summary:** The paper explores the issue of stalled training in neural networks using Direct Feedback Alignment (DFA), particularly when hidden layers are updated with fixed random projections of output errors. It identifies that the failure is linked to common-mode errors affecting the activation of hidden units and proposes a mean-covariance decomposition approach to analyze these interactions. Key findings include that adjustments to the readout layer and calibration of feedback can mitigate these stalls and improve learning efficiency across various network architectures and datasets.

### 8. Compact Documentation for Coding Agents: A Benchmark, an Optimizer, and Why It Does Not Transfer
**Authors:** Md Shohel Arman, Igor Molybog
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31587v1
**Summary:** This paper investigates whether natural-language documentation can help coding agents solve software issues effectively. The authors developed a benchmark to evaluate code descriptions and found that while they could create high-fidelity documentation that generalizes well, such documentation does not actually improve the agents’ ability to resolve real issues in code repositories. The key contribution includes a proposed benchmark and an optimizer, along with the insight that having the original source code is more beneficial than relying on documentation or context alone.

### 9. Trust Guided Decision Transformer
**Authors:** Chainesh Gautam, Raghuram Bharadwaj Diddigi, Chandramouli Kamanchi, Pankaj Dayama, Sumanta Mukherjee, Kameshwaran Sampath
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31586v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the problem of performance degradation in Decision Transformers during long rollouts due to context drift that leads to unreliable predictions. The authors introduce the Trust Guided Decision Transformer (TGDT), which evaluates recent context based on next state prediction errors to select trustworthy context for decision-making. Their experiments show that TGDT reduces prediction errors and improves decision performance compared to previous methods, demonstrating its effectiveness in enhancing the reliability of reinforcement learning tasks.

### 10. OC-GS: Gaussian Splatting for Irregular Turntable Capture
**Authors:** Jae Joong Lee, Bedrich Benes
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31572v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenges of reconstructing 3D objects from irregular turntable captures, where uneven rotation and dropped frames hinder traditional methods. The authors introduce OC-GS, a technique that refines camera angles while maintaining a consistent rotation axis to improve geometry reconstruction. The key finding is that this angle refinement significantly boosts reconstruction quality, achieving higher mean PSNR scores compared to existing methods, particularly when dealing with sparse and irregular image views.
