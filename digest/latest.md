## 2026-11-15

### 1. When Both Sides Adapt: The Challenges of Evaluating Interactive LLM Systems
**Authors:** Hannah Li
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17239v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of evaluating interactive language model systems, where users adapt their prompting strategies based on model responses, potentially skewing evaluation results. The authors propose two distinct methods of comparison, one based on a fixed set of prompts (common-distribution effect) and another on prompts shaped by user interactions (induced-distribution effect). Their key finding highlights that these two evaluation methods can lead to different model rankings, emphasizing the need to understand user behavior changes alongside model performance for accurate evaluation.

### 2. Al-Ghasali: A Computational Framework for Simulating Islamic Jurisprudential Rulings via Finite State Automata and Large Language Models
**Authors:** Elnaserledinellah Mahmoud Abdelwahab
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17235v1
**Summary:** The paper introduces Al Ghasali, a framework aimed at simulating Islamic jurisprudential rulings using deterministic finite state automata (FSA) and large language models (LLMs). It demonstrates that Fiqh chapters can be formally represented as FSAs that allow for quick verdict extraction, while ensuring compliance with Islamic legal principles through a controlled integration with LLMs. Key contributions include the design safety conditions that prevent errors in LLM outputs related to legal interpretations, ensuring accurate and reliable summaries of juristic content.

### 3. Two mathematical models of knowledge distillation
**Authors:** Audrey Xie, Ludwig Schmidt, John Duchi
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17230v1
**Summary:** The paper investigates the reasons behind the effectiveness of knowledge distillation in training machine learning models. The authors develop mathematical models that demonstrate distillation enhances optimization speed by improving the training landscape, rather than directly improving model quality. Their findings reveal that models trained with distillation converge faster than those trained on the original supervised objectives, a result supported by experimental validation on real datasets.

### 4. Linear Regression with Self-Selection Bias in Polynomial Time
**Authors:** Felix Zhou
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17228v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of estimating multiple linear regressors with self-selection bias in a polynomial time frame, particularly in the presence of correlated noise. The authors propose a new algorithm that leverages an improved understanding of covariate moments to achieve this, leading to a significant reduction in computational complexity compared to previous methods that relied on exhaustive searches. Their approach not only provides a first polynomial-time solution to this problem but also offers insights that may be applicable to other related latent-variable issues.

### 5. Sheaf-Inspired Structured Scoring Extensions of LightGCN for Collaborative Filtering
**Authors:** Ksenia Belkovskaya, Timur Lidzhiev, Maria Stebliy, Alexander Tarakanov
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17226v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of improving collaborative filtering by enhancing user and item representation learning through a sheaf-inspired scoring extension on top of a LightGCN model. The authors propose structured scoring methods that modify how user and item embeddings are transformed, rather than altering the graph propagation process itself. Their experiments show that these structured approaches yield modest improvements in ranking quality and varied impacts on catalog coverage across different datasets, though they do not demonstrate a direct advantage from the sheaf-based framework used.

### 6. Data Archive for 2D and 3D Electrical Impedance Tomography with moderate conductivity contrast phantoms
**Authors:** S. J. Hamilton, A. Hauptmann, A. Hänninen, V. Kolehmainen, J. Niskanen, T. Savolainen, J. Toivanen
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17225v1
**Summary:** The paper presents an open dataset of Electrical Impedance Tomography (EIT) measurements obtained from phantoms that simulate moderate conductivity contrasts, aimed at advancing image reconstruction algorithms. The dataset includes measurements from both 2D cylindrical and chest-shaped phantoms, as well as a 3D head-shaped phantom, and is accompanied by computational meshes and example codes for reconstruction. This resource is intended to support the development and validation of EIT techniques in biomedical imaging.

### 7. Combinatorial Bernoulli Factories with Polynomially Many Coins
**Authors:** Renato Paes Leme, Rad Niazadeh
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17223v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of creating efficient combinatorial Bernoulli factories, which convert coins with unknown biases into a random vertex of a polytope in a way that accurately reflects the biases. The authors present a single algorithm that operates in polynomial time with respect to the polytope's dimension and the sensitivity of the biases, achieving a significant reduction in the number of expected coin flips needed, especially compared to previous methods. Notably, their approach performs much better for perfect matchings, dramatically lowering the expected flips from an exponential to polynomial scale.

### 8. Scalable Conversational Assessment: Chat-Based Exams with a Two-Agent LLM Pipeline
**Authors:** Geoffrey Challen, Craig Zilles
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17220v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of scaling oral exams, which allow for in-depth student assessment but are difficult to manage with large numbers of students. It introduces a two-agent LLM (large language model) system that conducts interactive text-based conversations with students to assess their reasoning and argumentation skills, with one agent guiding the conversation and another evaluating progress. The system was successfully tested with simulated students and implemented in an AI literacy course, revealing insights for improving question generation and ensuring fair grading.

### 9. Introducing Feedback Delays to Non-Delayed Adversarial MDPs: Optimal $\sqrt S$ and $\sqrt K$ Dependencies in Tabular and Linear AMDPs
**Authors:** Aarush Kulkarni, Khang Nguyen, William Chang, Yan Dai
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17214v1
**Summary:** This paper addresses the challenge of minimizing regret in episodic Adversarial Markov Decision Processes (AMDPs), focusing on scenarios with unknown transitions and bandit feedback. The authors introduce a novel technique called feedback delays, enabling the estimation of the Q-function using future episodes while updating policies, which leads to significant improvements in regret bounds. Their approach finally resolves a long-standing gap in tabular AMDPs and provides optimal efficiency in both tabular and linear settings, even when considering feedback delays.

### 10. Theoretical and Empirical Analysis of Sparse Convolution
**Authors:** Keshvi Tuteja, Yu-Hsiang Tsai, Gregor Olenik, Markus Götz, Achim Streit, Hartwig Anzt, Charlotte Debus
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17212v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of efficiently using sparse convolutional kernels in deep neural networks on resource-limited devices. The authors conduct a performance analysis of sparse convolutions compared to dense ones and propose techniques to improve efficiency. Their results indicate that their approach can achieve up to 1.65 times faster inference on high-performance GPUs and up to 1.52 times on edge devices, highlighting the potential of sparse convolutions for more efficient neural network deployment.
