## 2026-08-11

### 1. Beyond Naturalness: Probing Automated Text-To-Speech Evaluators on Linguistically Grounded Dimensions
**Authors:** Oluwanifemi Bamgbose, Simon Rosen, Jash Shah, Lindsay Devon Brin, Hoang H Nguyen, Anke Koelzer, Rachel Hansen, Tara Bogavelli, Fanny Riols
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09930v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the inadequacy of automated Text-to-Speech (TTS) evaluation methods in reflecting human perceptions of speech quality, specifically beyond just "naturalness." The authors created a comprehensive framework that breaks down speech perception into 10 distinct dimensions and benchmarked various evaluation methods against this framework using a dataset of 860 annotated utterances. Key findings show that existing methods primarily focus on sound quality and fail to capture a wide range of linguistically relevant speech errors, highlighting the need for more targeted evaluation tools.

### 2. Multimodal Model Diffing for Feature Discovery and Control
**Authors:** Hunar Batra, Lachin Naghashyar, Ashkan Khakzar, Philip Torr, Christian Schroeder de Witt, Constantin Venhoff, Ronald Clark
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09928v1
**Summary:** The paper presents MMDiff, a framework for discovering and controlling features in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) that influence their behavior, which is challenging to audit and manipulate. Utilizing sparse autoencoders, MMDiff identifies and isolates specific features altered by multimodal training, allowing for targeted control and improvement in tasks related to spatial understanding and optical character recognition (OCR). The framework shows that it can effectively degrade undesired behaviors or enhance performance by adjusting these features, achieving notable improvements without negatively impacting other areas like visual question answering (VQA).

### 3. From Values to Benchmarks: Evaluating Large Language Models for Governmental Use in Dutch
**Authors:** Laurens Samson, Iva Gornishka, Gossa Lô, Yuki M. Asano, Sennay Ghebreab
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09925v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of evaluating large language models (LLMs) for use in Dutch government contexts, emphasizing the need for assessments that consider both public administration values and local language needs. The researchers developed the "Grip on LLMs" framework, which identifies six key evaluation dimensions and benchmarks over 30 models based on collaboration with domain experts and user research. The findings highlight that no single model performs best across all dimensions, revealing trade-offs between quality, environmental impact, and cost, and illustrating the distinct characteristics of factuality and honesty in model responses.

### 4. GENCO - A Unified Neural Solver Embedded in a Development Framework for Steady-State Grid Analysis
**Authors:** Alban Puech, Matteo Mazzonelli, Tamara R. Govindasamy, Mangaliso Mngomezulu, Héctor Maeso-García, Thomas Tolhurst, Javad Bayazi, Ali Moeini, Naomi Simumba, Celia Cintas, David Nelischer, Romeo Kienzler, Jonas Weiss, Anna Varbella, Florian Dörfler, Gabriela Hug, Martin Mevissen, Juan Bernabé-Moreno, François Mirallès, Hendrik F. Hamann, Etienne Vos, Thomas Brunschwiler
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09921v1
**Summary:** The paper introduces GENCO, a unified neural solver designed for steady-state transmission grid analysis, addressing the need for accurate power flow, optimal power flow, and state estimation in power systems while maintaining physical consistency. It features a shared architecture and is supported by the GridFM Development Framework, which simplifies data generation and training processes. Key results show that GENCO significantly outperforms traditional solvers in terms of speed and robustness, achieving faster calculations while ensuring high-quality estimates, thus making advanced power analysis more accessible to engineers.

### 5. DSLE: A Learning Environment for Dark Souls Boss Encounters
**Authors:** Derin Gezgin, Jim O'Connor, Tanner Goodwin, Gary B. Parker
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09902v1
**Summary:** The paper presents the Dark Souls Learning Environment (DSLE), a platform for evaluating AI agents in the challenging boss encounters of the game Dark Souls: Remastered. The researchers established a starting suite of five representative bosses (DSLE-5) and tested various AI strategies, including random, expert, and deep reinforcement learning methods, revealing that none of the agents were able to successfully defeat most of the bosses, highlighting the difficulty of learning in such a complex environment. Key findings include the limited success of traditional AI methods and insights into their performance through metrics like survival time and damage dealt rather than just win rates.

### 6. Decoding-Level Taboo: A Diagnostic Stress Test for LLM Robustness
**Authors:** Tadanobu Chuyo Kamijo, Ori Rottenstreich, Javier Conde, Gonzalo Martínez, Pedro Reviriego
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09900v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the issue of large language models (LLMs) performing well under ideal conditions but struggling in real-world scenarios due to various constraints. To evaluate LLM robustness, the authors introduce Decoding-Level Taboo, a diagnostic stress test that disrupts normal token selection during generation, prompting models to navigate around obstacles. Their findings indicate that larger and better-aligned models show improved robustness when faced with these challenges, and the Taboo method offers a way to create diverse test datasets and assess model reliability before deployment.

### 7. Fairness in Link Prediction Beyond Demographic Parity: A Reproducibility Study
**Authors:** Valentijn Oldenburg, Floris de Kam, Stef de Wildt, Jarno Nilson Balk
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09899v1
**Summary:** This study addresses the inadequacy of the common fairness metric, demographic parity, in link prediction, which fails to account for ranking disparities between subgroups. The researchers demonstrate that their proposed rank-aware metric, Normalized Discounted KL-divergence (NDKL), reveals biases overlooked by demographic parity. Additionally, they validate the effectiveness of the MORAL post-processing method in improving fairness without significantly affecting utility across various datasets.

### 8. Consilience for Verifier-Free Test-Time Scaling
**Authors:** Lecheng Kong, Like Hui, Haitao Mao, Jun Huan
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09898v1
**Summary:** This paper addresses the challenge of verifier-free test-time scaling (VF-TTS) in enhancing Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning, particularly highlighting the limitations of existing confidence-based methods that can lead to incorrectly confident outputs on complex tasks. The authors introduce a new framework called consilience, which emphasizes the importance of a specific pattern in confidence over time to encourage exploration in problem-solving. Through extensive experiments, they demonstrate that consilience significantly outperforms previous methods, providing a more effective approach to navigating complex reasoning tasks.

### 9. Fusion Training for Mathematical Generalization in Large Language Models
**Authors:** Congfeng Cao, Pengyu Zhang, Jelke Bloem
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09893v1
**Summary:** The paper investigates how to effectively train large language models using a method called Thinking Mode Fusion (TMF) to improve their ability to solve mathematical problems. By analyzing different combinations of training schedules and data ratios for thinking and non-thinking modes, the authors find that too much non-thinking supervision can negatively affect thinking mode performance. Their work highlights the trade-offs involved in TMF training, offering insights for optimizing training settings in future models.

### 10. BDH-CQ: In-Context Learning with Recurrent Latent Reasoning
**Authors:** Björn Engdahl, Adrian Kosowski, Jan Chorowski, Zuzanna Stamirowska, Przemysław Uznański, Junlin Jiang, Rohan Phadke, Remigiusz Kinas, Richard Zhong
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09888v1
**Summary:** BDH-CQ is a new reasoning model designed to enhance in-context learning by utilizing recurrent latent reasoning, allowing it to iteratively solve queries based on continuously updated information. Evaluated on the ARC-AGI-1 benchmark, the model achieves a new cost-efficient state of the art by reaching a pass rate of 29.5% while maintaining a low inference cost, surpassing previous performance metrics.
