## 2026-08-12

### 1. Surgical WAM: A World-Action Model for Data-Efficient Surgical Robot Learning
**Authors:** Wenrui Bao, Tianyun Jiang, Zhiben Chen, Ser-Nam Lim, Peter D. Peng, Yuzhang Shang
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11204v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of learning surgical manipulation policies due to the lack of sufficient action-labeled demonstrations, which are expensive to obtain. To tackle this, the authors propose the Surgical World-Action Model (Surgical WAM), which leverages action-free endoscopic video to learn visual dynamics and improves performance through fine-tuning with limited action-labeled data. The results show that this approach significantly enhances the success rates of simulated surgical tasks, demonstrating the effectiveness of utilizing video pretraining for data-efficient learning in surgical robotics.

### 2. ConVAWG: A Retrieval-Grounded Framework for Controlled Synthetic Dialogue Generation in Violence Against Women and Girls
**Authors:** Chen Lyu, Xingwei Tan, Simon Cullen, Shelley Wilson, Lois Arthurs, Arshad Jhumka, Gabriele Pergola
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11200v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of studying conversations related to Violence Against Women and Girls (VAWG) in contexts where real data is scarce and sensitive. The authors developed a framework called ConVAWG, which creates synthetic multi-turn dialogue scenarios grounded in retrieved cases and demographic data, applying targeted controls to manage toxicity in the dialogues. The key contribution is the release of over 6,000 high-quality synthetic dialogue events that effectively model VAWG dynamics, enabling further research and understanding of this critical issue.

### 3. Beyond a Bag of Features: Set-Level Instability in Sparse Autoencoders
**Authors:** Nikolai Bolik, Lennart Stöpler, Artur Andrzejak
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11197v1
**Summary:** The paper investigates how representations from sparse autoencoders (SAEs) compare to human understanding of category boundaries and typicality. The authors use a new similarity measure based on the overlap of active SAE latent sets, revealing that these sets do not align with human categorizations as effectively as dense embeddings do. Their findings suggest that the features in SAEs do not form simple compositional structures, challenging the traditional "bag-of-features" approach in representing concepts.

### 4. Long-Horizon AI Research for Grothendieck Constant: A Case Study in Human-AI Mathematical Collaboration
**Authors:** Alan Li, Rahul Saha, Anton Xue, Swarat Chaudhuri, Adam Klivans, Pravesh K Kothari, Raghu Meka
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11195v1
**Summary:** This paper explores the effective use of AI in mathematical research, focusing on improving the bounds of the Grothendieck constant, which is important in understanding the relationship between combinatorial problems and their continuous relaxations. By employing an AI research system, the authors successfully tightened the known bounds for the constant, demonstrating that AI can generate novel insights in mathematics. The study also highlights the strengths and weaknesses of using AI in this context, providing guidance for future human-AI collaborations in mathematical exploration.

### 5. Test-Time Self-Evolving GUI Visual Grounding via Reflection-Guided On-Policy Self-Distillation
**Authors:** Shiyu Xuan, Zechao Li
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11191v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the limitation of existing GUI agents that cannot adapt to new interfaces after deployment. The authors introduce a framework that enables these agents to enhance their performance in real-time through exploration, evaluation, and self-reflection without requiring human annotations. Their approach, which combines Reflection-Guided On-Policy Self-Distillation, shows a notable accuracy improvement of 7.4% over baseline models, marking a significant advancement in post-deployment adaptability for GUI visual grounding.

### 6. How to Verify Consistency of Probabilistic Claims
**Authors:** Orr Paradise, Oliver Richardson, Yoshua Bengio, Shafi Goldwasser
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11181v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the problem of verifying the self-consistency of probabilistic claims made by predictive models in a way that can be performed efficiently. The authors develop an interactive proof system that allows a verifier to assess the consistency of predictions from a pair of circuits with respect to an alleged probability distribution by evaluating only a small number of points. A key contribution is the establishment of a complexity-theoretic framework that supports this verification approach, paving the way for models to demonstrate their own consistency.

### 7. A Quantum Roadmap for Softmax Attention: Exact Born-Rule Analogs for Softmax Attention on the Probability Simplex
**Authors:** Eric A. F. Reinhardt, Adam J. Hauser
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11173v1
**Summary:** The paper presents a quantum framework for implementing softmax attention, which is crucial for AI models like Transformers, specifically when inputs and outputs are constrained to the probability simplex. By utilizing quantum mechanics, the authors demonstrate that softmax attention can be realized through Hadamard-test statistics and show that attention scores can be derived from quantum measurements. A significant contribution is the establishment of an exact correspondence between softmax attention and quantum processes, providing a precise model that remains valid under certain conditions.

### 8. From Interpretability to Control: Insights from Six Years of the TrustNLP Workshop
**Authors:** Rahul Gupta, Abhinav Mohanty, Anaelia Ovalle, Anil Ramakrishna, Anubrata Das, Apurv Verma, Jwala Dhamala, Ninareh Mehrabi, Tharindu Kumarage, Yada Pruksachatkun, Yang Trista Cao, Kai-Wei Chang, Aram Galstyan
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11171v1
**Summary:** The paper reviews six years of the TrustNLP Workshop, which focuses on improving the trustworthiness of natural language processing models, transitioning from merely interpreting models to actively ensuring their safety and reliability. By analyzing 144 proceedings papers, the authors highlight shifts in research focus, noting that truthfulness is rapidly becoming a priority, while fairness remains consistently important. The findings provide valuable guidance for future research directions in the field.

### 9. MultiModal Code-Switching: Interleaving Visual Objects into Language for Explicit Object-Level Alignment
**Authors:** Changhao Xiang, Shangyu Xing, Zhen Wu, Jianbing Zhang, Xinyu Dai
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11167v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the challenge of aligning visual objects with text in multimodal large language models, which often struggle with referential ambiguity when only using global image representations. To tackle this, the authors introduce MultiModal Code-Switching (MMCS), a new pretraining method that replaces textual entities with their corresponding visual objects, facilitating better local vision-language grounding. The key finding is that MMCS demonstrates high data efficiency, achieving performance comparable to models trained on significantly larger datasets, while also enhancing visual grounding and perception across different model sizes.

### 10. Hierarchical Empirical-Bayes Naive Bayes: Minimax Smoothing and Calibration with AODE Extension
**Authors:** Nguyen Thai Anh, Truong Viet Vu, Tran Thien Thanh, Vo Nguyen Quoc Bao, Ngo Hoang Tu
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11162v1
**Summary:** The paper addresses the limitations of traditional smoothing methods in Naive Bayes classifiers, which can introduce bias in high-cardinality categorical data. The authors introduce a hierarchical empirical-Bayes approach that adaptively learns smoothing strengths for class-feature probabilities using data, while maintaining closed-form inference. Their method, HEB-NB, significantly improves performance on various benchmarks, achieving up to a 22.1% reduction in log-loss and enhancing calibration accuracy compared to standard techniques.
